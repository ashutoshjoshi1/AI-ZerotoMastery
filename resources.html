Further resources

A short, opinionated list. Each item earns its place; you do not need all of them. Start with the one that matches the part of the course you found hardest. Model names, prices and library APIs change quickly, so for anything product-specific prefer the official documentation over books. Part numbers are the ten parts of the course; part IX, the wider field, is where most of the newer entries sit.

Books#

Book Best for Course parts
Chip Huyen, AI Engineering (2025) Building applications on foundation models: evaluation, RAG, agents, inference VI to VIII, X
Chip Huyen, Designing Machine Learning Systems (2022) Production ML: data, deployment, monitoring III, VIII

Courses and video series#

  • Andrej Karpathy, Neural Networks: Zero to Hero (YouTube): micrograd, makemore, building GPT and its tokenizer from scratch. The best complement to parts IV and V.
  • 3Blue1Brown: Essence of Linear Algebra, Essence of Calculus, and the neural networks series including transformers and attention.
  • fast.ai, Practical Deep Learning for Coders: top-down, code-first deep learning.
  • Andrew Ng, Machine Learning Specialization (Coursera) and the DeepLearning.AI short courses on LLM topics (RAG, agents, evaluation, fine-tuning).
  • Hugging Face courses: the LLM course, the agents course, the MCP course, the diffusion course and the Deep RL course (part IX); hands-on with the libraries you will use.
  • Stanford CS224N (NLP with deep learning) and CS336 (language modelling from scratch): lecture videos and assignments online, for going deep on parts V and VII.
  • Stanford CS231n (deep learning for computer vision): the standard route into part IX's vision lesson, with assignments.
  • David Silver's reinforcement learning lectures (UCL and DeepMind, YouTube): the lecture companion to Sutton and Barto.
  • Stanford CS224W (machine learning with graphs): for the graph half of lesson 28.
  • ARENA (arena.education) and Neel Nanda's "getting started in mechanistic interpretability": exercise-driven material for lesson 30.

Blogs and writing by practitioners#

  • Anthropic engineering blog: "Building effective agents", "Effective context engineering for AI agents", "Introducing Contextual Retrieval" and posts on tool design and evaluation.
  • Transformer Circuits (transformer-circuits.pub): Anthropic's interpretability research, from "Toy Models of Superposition" to the sparse-autoencoder and circuit-tracing work behind lesson 30.
  • Lilian Weng (lilianweng.github.io): thorough surveys of agents, reinforcement learning, diffusion, hallucination and more.
  • Jay Alammar: illustrated explanations of transformers and LLMs.
  • Simon Willison (simonwillison.net): hands-on LLM tooling and prompt-injection security.
  • Eugene Yan (eugeneyan.com): patterns for building LLM products, evaluation, recommendation systems.
  • Hamel Husain (hamel.dev): evaluation and fine-tuning in practice.
  • Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (interconnects.ai): post-training, reasoning models and open-model releases, read critically.
  • Sebastian Raschka (magazine.sebastianraschka.com): research summaries and implementation notes.

Datasets for practice#

  • scikit-learn built-ins (breast cancer, digits, diabetes) and OpenML for classical ML.
  • Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10 for image classification; Oxford-IIIT Pet and COCO for segmentation and detection.
  • Tiny Shakespeare and Project Gutenberg texts for language modelling.
  • Hugging Face Datasets hub for almost everything else, including instruction, preference and reasoning datasets.
  • Gymnasium environments (CartPole, LunarLander, the Atari games) for reinforcement learning.
  • MovieLens for recommenders, and Cora and the other datasets that ship with PyTorch Geometric for graphs.
  • Public environmental data (air-quality networks, weather stations, satellite products) and the M5 forecasting competition data for time series, forecasting and anomaly-detection capstones.
