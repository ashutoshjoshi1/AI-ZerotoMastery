AI engineering, from zero to mastery

33 lessons in 111 sessions of about an hour each. Python and maths first, then machine learning, deep learning and transformers, then the craft of building, evaluating and shipping systems on large language models, and finally the wider field of AI. Read on any screen; practise in a Jupyter notebook on your computer.

How the course works#

Every lesson is split into sessions of about an hour. A session is one sitting: read the section, run the code, do the hands-on exercise. Inside each lesson a divider marks where one session ends and the next begins, with a checkbox to tick it off. The quiz at the end of a lesson comes once you have finished all its sessions. The daily schedule lays the whole course out day by day, and its checkboxes record the same progress.

The order matters. Parts I to V build the mental model: Python, the maths, classical machine learning, neural networks and the transformer. Part VI is the day-to-day craft of an AI engineer: calling models, prompting, reasoning models, retrieval, agents, context engineering and evaluation. Part VII changes models and runs them efficiently: fine-tuning, post-training and inference. Part VIII is about running all of that in production. Part IX covers the wider field of AI beyond language models, and part X pulls everything together into system design, capstone projects and a portfolio.

If you already know a topic, skim the lesson, do the hands-on exercises and the quiz, and move on. If the exercises are easy and the quiz is clean, you have earned the skip.

Read on your phone, practise at your computer#

The lessons are written to be read anywhere. The code is meant to be run on a computer. The course is built for switching between the two.

  1. Read on any screen. Open https://ashutoshjoshi1.github.io/AI-ZerotoMastery/ on your phone. In Safari use Share, Add to Home Screen; in Chrome use Install app. It then opens like an app, and every lesson you open is saved for reading offline. The Aa button sets the text size, typeface and theme.
  2. Practise at your computer. Clone the repository, open the notebooks folder in JupyterLab or VS Code, and run the notebook with the same number as the lesson. Each exercise in a lesson is labelled At your computer and names the notebook cell to use. No setup on the day? Every lesson also has an Open in Colab link.
  3. Keep one set of ticks. Progress is saved in each browser separately. The Aa button gives you a sync link: open it on your other device and its ticks are merged in.

A good rhythm: read a session on your phone during the day, then sit down at your computer in the evening, open the notebook and do that session's exercise. The lesson page remembers where you stopped reading.

What you need#

  • A computer with Python 3.11 or newer. Lesson 00 walks through the setup.
  • A free Google Colab account (or any machine with an NVIDIA GPU) for the deep learning, fine-tuning and inference lessons. Everything else runs on a laptop CPU.
  • An API key for a hosted model provider for part VI. A few US dollars of credit covers every exercise in the course.
  • A Git repository for your notes and code. Treat it as your lab notebook; it becomes your portfolio.

Companion notebooks#

Every lesson has a Jupyter notebook in the notebooks folder with all of the lesson's code ready to run and an empty cell for each hands-on exercise. Complete, runnable solutions are in notebooks/solutions. notebooks/README.md explains how to run them locally or on Colab, and which ones need a GPU or an API key.

Habits that make this stick#

Type the code rather than pasting it, at least through part V. Typing forces you to read every line, and the typos you fix teach you more than the code that ran first time.

Close the page and explain it back. At the end of each session, write three or four sentences in your learning log describing what you learned, without looking. If you cannot, reread the section. This is retrieval practice, and it is the single most effective study technique we know of.

Build something small every week. The hands-on exercises are designed to be extended. Change the dataset, swap the model, break it on purpose, and write down what happened.

The route

I Orientation

Set up your workbench and see the whole map before you start walking. 2 sessions

  1. 00 Welcome and your AI workbench2 sessions · 20 min read

    What an AI engineer actually does, how the field fits together, how to study it an hour at a time, and a working Python setup you will use for the rest of the course.

II Foundations

Python, numerical computing and the maths every later lesson leans on. 7 sessions

  1. 01 Python for AI work3 sessions · 25 min read

    The Python you actually need for AI, then NumPy for fast array maths and pandas plus Matplotlib for loading, cleaning and plotting real data.

  2. 02 The maths behind AI4 sessions · 15 min read

    Just enough linear algebra, calculus and probability to understand what every model is doing, with each idea turned straight into NumPy code.

III Machine learning

How models learn from data, and how to tell whether they really did. 7 sessions

  1. 03 Machine learning fundamentals3 sessions · 15 min read

    What it means for a model to learn, the workflow every ML project follows, why models overfit and how to stop them, and how to measure performance honestly.

  2. 04 Classical machine learning4 sessions · 20 min read

    The algorithms that still run much of the world, from linear and logistic regression built by hand to random forests, gradient boosting, clustering and PCA, finishing with an end-to-end scikit-learn project.

IV Deep learning

Neural networks from first principles, then the same ideas in PyTorch. 10 sessions

  1. 05 Neural networks from scratch4 sessions · 15 min read

    Build a multilayer neural network in plain NumPy, derive and implement backpropagation yourself, then learn the practical tricks (initialisation, Adam, normalisation, dropout) that make deep networks train.

  2. 06 Deep learning with PyTorch4 sessions · 20 min read

    Move from hand-written NumPy to PyTorch, the framework behind most modern AI research and open models; learn tensors, autograd and the standard training loop, then train CNNs and fine-tune a pretrained network.

  3. 07 Text as numbers, sequences and embeddings2 sessions · 15 min read

    How text becomes vectors whose geometry captures meaning, how recurrent networks read sequences one step at a time, and the bottleneck that motivated attention.

V Transformers and LLMs

Attention, the transformer block, and how large language models are made. 9 sessions

  1. 08 Attention and the transformer5 sessions · 25 min read

    The architecture behind every modern LLM, built up piece by piece: self-attention in NumPy, masking and multiple heads, the transformer block, then a small GPT you train yourself in PyTorch.

  2. 09 How large language models are built4 sessions · 20 min read

    The full life of an LLM, from tokenizer to pretraining at scale, then instruction tuning, RLHF, DPO and reasoning training, ending with how decoding settings shape what the model says and why it sometimes makes things up.

VI Building with LLMs

The core craft: APIs, prompts, reasoning models, retrieval, agents, context engineering and evaluation. 28 sessions

  1. 10 Working with LLM APIs3 sessions · 40 min read

    Call hosted and local models from code like a professional, covering messages and system prompts, streaming, reliable structured output, errors and retries, token costs, caching, batches and concurrency, then build a streaming command-line chatbot.

  2. 11 Prompt engineering3 sessions · 25 min read

    Write prompts that work reliably, not just once, through clear instructions, context, examples and structure, along with reasoning, chaining and voting techniques, and treat prompts as tested, versioned code.

  3. 12 Reasoning models and test-time compute3 sessions · 60 min read

    Use models that think before they answer: what test-time compute buys, how reasoning models are trained, how to call them with thinking and effort controls, how to build self-consistency, best-of-N and verifier loops yourself, and how to prompt, evaluate and route reasoning systems without overpaying.

  4. 13 Embeddings and vector search3 sessions · 25 min read

    Turn sentences and documents into vectors that capture meaning, search them by similarity from scratch and with FAISS or a vector database, and understand the approximate-nearest-neighbour indexes that make it fast at scale.

  5. 14 Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)5 sessions · 25 min read

    Make a model answer from your own documents, with citations, by building the full pipeline without a framework (parsing, chunking, hybrid retrieval, reranking and grounded generation), then measure it and fix what it gets wrong.

  6. 15 Agents, tool use and MCP5 sessions · 25 min read

    Give models the ability to act by writing the tool-use loop by hand, designing tools models use well, choosing between workflows and autonomous agents, and building an MCP server, then add the guardrails and evaluation that make agents safe to deploy.

  7. 16 Context engineering and memory3 sessions · 85 min read

    Decide what the model sees on every call: measure how budgets, position and distractors affect quality, keep long conversations and agent runs within budget with selection, compression, ordering, isolation and caching, then give assistants memory that persists across sessions without leaking or being poisoned.

  8. 17 Evaluating LLM systems3 sessions · 40 min read

    The discipline that separates demos from products, covering golden datasets, code-based graders and LLM judges done properly, grading agents and conversations, statistics that tell you whether a change really helped, an evaluation harness, regression tests, and the limits of public benchmarks.

VII Fine-tuning and inference

Change what a model knows and how it behaves, then make it fast and cheap to run: supervised fine-tuning, preference tuning and distillation, and the mechanics of LLM inference. 6 sessions

  1. 18 Fine-tuning language models4 sessions · 30 min read

    Learn when fine-tuning is the right tool and when it is not, understand LoRA and QLoRA down to the matrix shapes and memory budget, fine-tune a small open model on your own task and measure it against a prompted baseline, then see where preference tuning, distillation and adapter serving take you next.

  2. 19 Post-training in depth: data, preference tuning, RL fine-tuning, distillation and merging1 sessions · 5 min read

    Everything after supervised fine-tuning: building good training data, reward models and DPO, RL fine-tuning with verifiable rewards, distillation and model merging.

  3. 20 LLM inference in depth1 sessions · 5 min read

    What happens between a prompt arriving and tokens streaming back: prefill and decode, the KV cache, batching, quantisation, speculative decoding and inference engines.

VIII Production

Serving, scaling, securing and extending AI systems in the real world. 10 sessions

  1. 21 Deployment, serving and LLMOps4 sessions · 35 min read

    Take an AI system from notebook to production by building a streaming API service, choosing how to serve open models within a GPU memory budget, controlling latency and cost, and making everything observable: tracing, drift monitoring, cost dashboards, and rollouts gated on evals.

  2. 22 Safety, security and responsible AI3 sessions · 15 min read

    Understand how LLM systems get attacked and how they fail (prompt injection, jailbreaks, data leakage, excessive agency), defend them in depth, and handle hallucination, bias, privacy and regulation responsibly.

  3. 23 Multimodal AI: vision, documents, speech and image generation3 sessions · 15 min read

    Extend everything you know beyond text, from vision transformers and CLIP to sending images and documents to vision-language models, transcribing speech, and understanding how diffusion models generate images.

IX The wider field

The rest of AI beyond language models: search and planning, reinforcement learning, generative models, vision, time series and graphs, training at scale, and interpretability. Each lesson stands on its own, so take them in order or pick the ones your work needs. 23 sessions

  1. 24 Search, planning and classical AI3 sessions · 75 min read

    The half of AI that is not learned from data: search mazes and road maps, find routes with A*, schedule with a constraint solver, play games with minimax and Monte Carlo tree search, reason with rules and probabilities, and see how AlphaZero and LLM agents combine these ideas with learned models.

  2. 25 Reinforcement learning4 sessions · 60 min read

    Learn how agents improve from rewards instead of labels: build bandits, value iteration, Q-learning and a policy-gradient agent from scratch, then see how the same ideas (PPO, GRPO and verifiable rewards) post-train today's language models.

  3. 26 Generative models: autoencoders, VAEs, GANs, flows and diffusion3 sessions · 70 min read

    See every deep generative model as one idea, learning a probability distribution and sampling from it, then build an autoencoder, a VAE, a GAN, a normalising flow, a diffusion model and a flow-matching model from scratch on a laptop CPU, and learn how to judge what they produce.

  4. 27 Computer vision: detection, segmentation and beyond3 sessions · 80 min read

    Go beyond labelling whole images: implement IoU, non-maximum suppression and mAP yourself, train a small object detector and a U-Net segmenter on a CPU, then put pretrained, promptable and open-vocabulary models to work, track objects through video and get vision models ready to deploy.

  5. 28 Time series, recommender systems and graphs4 sessions · 75 min read

    Three kinds of data that break the independent-rows assumption: forecast with honest baselines and walk-forward backtests, build and evaluate recommenders from matrix factorisation to two-tower retrieval, and learn on graphs with PageRank, node embeddings and a graph neural network written from scratch.

  6. 29 Training at scale: GPUs, parallelism and efficient architectures3 sessions · 55 min read

    See what really limits training speed and memory on accelerators, how data, tensor and pipeline parallelism and sharding split a model across thousands of GPUs, and which architectural ideas (FlashAttention, mixture of experts, state-space models) make frontier models affordable, all through measurements and simulations you run on a laptop.

  7. 30 Interpretability and explainability3 sessions · 85 min read

    Find out why a model made a prediction, what it has learned and how far to trust the answer: coefficients, permutation importance and Shapley values for tabular models, gradients for neural networks, and probes, the logit lens, activation patching and sparse autoencoders inside a small transformer.

X Mastery

System design, capstone projects, and turning skill into a career. 9 sessions

  1. 31 Designing AI systems3 sessions · 25 min read

    Pull the whole course together into a repeatable way of designing AI systems, apply it to worked case studies that mix language models with the rest of the toolbox, and learn to read papers and stay current without drowning.

  2. 32 Capstone projects and your AI engineering career6 sessions · 30 min read

    Prove your skills by building one end-to-end system from seven capstone specifications with rubrics, then turn the work into a portfolio, prepare for interviews with worked questions, and plan your next ninety days.
