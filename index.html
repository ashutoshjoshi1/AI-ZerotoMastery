AI engineering, from zero to mastery

22 lessons in 80 sessions of about an hour each. Python and maths first, then machine learning, deep learning and transformers, then the craft of building, evaluating and shipping systems on top of large language models.

How the course works#

Every lesson is split into sessions of about an hour. A session is one sitting: read the section, run the code, do the hands-on exercise, and answer the quiz at the end of the lesson once you have finished all its sessions. The daily schedule lays the whole thing out day by day, and the checkboxes there and at the top of each lesson record the same progress.

The order matters. Parts I to V build the mental model: Python, the maths, classical machine learning, neural networks, and the transformer. Part VI is the day-to-day craft of an AI engineer: calling models, prompting, retrieval, agents, evaluation and fine-tuning. Part VII is about running all of that in production, and part VIII pulls it together into system design, capstone projects and a portfolio.

If you already know a topic, skim the lesson, do the hands-on exercise and the quiz, and move on. If the exercise is easy and the quiz is clean, you have earned the skip.

What you need#

  • A computer with Python 3.11 or newer. Lesson 00 walks through the setup.
  • A free Google Colab account (or any machine with an NVIDIA GPU) for the deep learning and fine-tuning lessons. Everything else runs on a laptop CPU.
  • An API key for a hosted model provider for part VI. A few US dollars of credit covers every exercise in the course.
  • A Git repository for your notes and code. Treat it as your lab notebook; it becomes your portfolio.

Companion notebooks#

Every lesson has a Jupyter notebook in the notebooks folder with all of the lesson's code ready to run and an empty cell for each hands-on exercise. Complete, runnable solutions are in notebooks/solutions. Open them in JupyterLab or VS Code, or upload them to Google Colab for a free GPU; notebooks/README.md explains both. Read a section of the lesson here, then run the matching cells there.

Habits that make this stick#

Type the code rather than pasting it, at least through part V. Typing forces you to read every line, and the typos you fix teach you more than the code that ran first time.

Close the page and explain it back. At the end of each session, write three or four sentences in your learning log describing what you learned, without looking. If you cannot, reread the section. This is retrieval practice, and it is the single most effective study technique we know of.

Build something small every week. The hands-on exercises are designed to be extended. Change the dataset, swap the model, break it on purpose, and write down what happened.

Progress is stored in this browser only. If you clear your browser data or switch machines, your ticks will not follow you. The course itself is plain HTML, so you can keep it in your repository and open it anywhere.

The route

I Orientation

Set up your workbench and see the whole map before you start walking. 2 sessions

  1. 00 Welcome and your AI workbench2 sessions

    What an AI engineer actually does, how the field fits together, how to study it an hour at a time, and a working Python setup you will use for the rest of the course.

II Foundations

Python, numerical computing and the maths every later lesson leans on. 7 sessions

  1. 01 Python for AI work3 sessions

    The Python you actually need for AI, then NumPy for fast array maths and pandas plus Matplotlib for loading, cleaning and plotting real data.

  2. 02 The maths behind AI4 sessions

    Just enough linear algebra, calculus and probability to understand what every model is doing, with each idea turned straight into NumPy code.

III Machine learning

How models learn from data, and how to tell whether they really did. 7 sessions

  1. 03 Machine learning fundamentals3 sessions

    What it means for a model to learn, the workflow every ML project follows, why models overfit and how to stop them, and how to measure performance honestly.

  2. 04 Classical machine learning4 sessions

    The algorithms that still run much of the world, from linear and logistic regression built by hand to random forests, gradient boosting, clustering and PCA, finishing with an end-to-end scikit-learn project.

IV Deep learning

Neural networks from first principles, then the same ideas in PyTorch. 10 sessions

  1. 05 Neural networks from scratch4 sessions

    Build a multilayer neural network in plain NumPy, derive and implement backpropagation yourself, then learn the practical tricks (initialisation, Adam, normalisation, dropout) that make deep networks train.

  2. 06 Deep learning with PyTorch4 sessions

    Move from hand-written NumPy to PyTorch, the framework behind most modern AI research and open models; learn tensors, autograd and the standard training loop, then train CNNs and fine-tune a pretrained network.

  3. 07 Text as numbers, sequences and embeddings2 sessions

    How text becomes vectors whose geometry captures meaning, how recurrent networks read sequences one step at a time, and the bottleneck that motivated attention.

V Transformers and LLMs

Attention, the transformer block, and how large language models are made. 9 sessions

  1. 08 Attention and the transformer5 sessions

    The architecture behind every modern LLM, built up piece by piece: self-attention in NumPy, masking and multiple heads, the transformer block, then a small GPT you train yourself in PyTorch.

  2. 09 How large language models are built4 sessions

    The full life of an LLM, from tokenizer to pretraining at scale, then instruction tuning, RLHF, DPO and reasoning training, ending with how decoding settings shape what the model says and why it sometimes makes things up.

VI Building with LLMs

The core craft: APIs, prompts, retrieval, agents, evaluation and fine-tuning. 26 sessions

  1. 10 Working with LLM APIs3 sessions

    Call hosted and local models from code like a professional, covering messages and system prompts, streaming, reliable structured output, errors and retries, token costs, caching and concurrency, then build a streaming command-line chatbot.

  2. 11 Prompt engineering3 sessions

    Write prompts that work reliably, not just once, through clear instructions, context, examples and structure, along with reasoning and chaining techniques, and treat prompts as tested, versioned code.

  3. 12 Embeddings and vector search3 sessions

    Turn sentences and documents into vectors that capture meaning, search them by similarity from scratch and with FAISS or a vector database, and understand the approximate-nearest-neighbour indexes that make it fast at scale.

  4. 13 Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)5 sessions

    Make a model answer from your own documents, with citations, by building the full pipeline without a framework (parsing, chunking, hybrid retrieval, reranking and grounded generation), then measure it and fix what it gets wrong.

  5. 14 Agents, tool use and MCP5 sessions

    Give models the ability to act by writing the tool-use loop by hand, designing tools models use well, choosing between workflows and autonomous agents, and building an MCP server, then add the guardrails and evaluation that make agents safe to deploy.

  6. 15 Evaluating LLM systems3 sessions

    The discipline that separates demos from products, covering golden datasets, code-based graders and LLM judges done properly, statistics that tell you whether a change really helped, an evaluation harness, regression tests, and the limits of public benchmarks.

  7. 16 Fine-tuning language models4 sessions

    Learn when fine-tuning is the right tool and when it is not, understand LoRA and QLoRA down to the matrix shapes and memory budget, fine-tune a small open model on your own task, then go further with DPO, distillation and adapter serving.

VII Production

Serving, scaling, securing and extending AI systems in the real world. 10 sessions

  1. 17 Deployment, serving and LLMOps4 sessions

    Take an AI system from notebook to production by building a streaming API service, serving open models efficiently with quantisation and the right memory budget, controlling latency and cost, and making everything observable.

  2. 18 Safety, security and responsible AI3 sessions

    Understand how LLM systems get attacked and how they fail (prompt injection, jailbreaks, data leakage, excessive agency), defend them in depth, and handle hallucination, bias, privacy and regulation responsibly.

  3. 19 Multimodal AI: vision, documents, speech and image generation3 sessions

    Extend everything you know beyond text, from vision transformers and CLIP to sending images and documents to vision-language models, transcribing speech, and understanding how diffusion models generate images.

VIII Mastery

System design, capstone projects, and turning skill into a career. 9 sessions

  1. 20 Designing AI systems3 sessions

    Pull the whole course together into a repeatable way of designing AI systems, apply it to three worked case studies, and learn to read papers and stay current without drowning.

  2. 21 Capstone projects and your AI engineering career6 sessions

    Prove your skills by building one end-to-end system from five capstone specifications with rubrics, then turn the work into a portfolio, prepare for interviews with worked questions, and plan your next ninety days.
