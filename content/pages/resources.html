A short, opinionated list. Each item earns its place; you do not need all of them. Start with the one that matches the part of the course you found hardest. Model names, prices and library APIs change quickly, so for anything product-specific prefer the official documentation over books.

Courses and video series

  • Andrej Karpathy, Neural Networks: Zero to Hero (YouTube): micrograd, makemore, building GPT and its tokenizer from scratch. The best complement to parts IV and V.
  • 3Blue1Brown: Essence of Linear Algebra, Essence of Calculus, and the neural networks series including transformers and attention.
  • fast.ai, Practical Deep Learning for Coders: top-down, code-first deep learning.
  • Andrew Ng, Machine Learning Specialization (Coursera) and the DeepLearning.AI short courses on LLM topics (RAG, agents, evaluation, fine-tuning).
  • Hugging Face courses: the LLM course, the diffusion course, the agents course; hands-on with the libraries you will use.
  • Stanford CS224N (NLP with deep learning) and CS336 (language modelling from scratch): lecture videos and assignments online, for going deep.

Blogs and writing by practitioners

  • Anthropic engineering blog: "Building effective agents", "Introducing Contextual Retrieval" and posts on tool design and evaluation.
  • Lilian Weng (lilianweng.github.io): thorough surveys of agents, hallucination, diffusion and more.
  • Jay Alammar: illustrated explanations of transformers and LLMs.
  • Simon Willison (simonwillison.net): hands-on LLM tooling and prompt-injection security.
  • Eugene Yan (eugeneyan.com): patterns for building LLM products, evaluation, recommendation systems.
  • Hamel Husain (hamel.dev): evaluation and fine-tuning in practice.
  • Sebastian Raschka (magazine.sebastianraschka.com): research summaries and implementation notes.

Datasets for practice

  • scikit-learn built-ins (breast cancer, digits, diabetes) and OpenML for classical ML.
  • Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10 for image classification.
  • Tiny Shakespeare and Project Gutenberg texts for language modelling.
  • Hugging Face Datasets hub for almost everything else, including instruction and preference datasets.
  • Public environmental data (air-quality networks, weather stations, satellite products) for time-series and anomaly-detection capstones.
